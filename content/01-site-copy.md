## ABOUT `/about`

### Hero
**Eyebrow:** ABOUT SELV
**Headline:** Built by people who needed it.

SELV didn't start as a company. It started as a question one of us wrote in a notebook at 2 a.m., after another day of performing confidence we didn't have: *if the inner voice is learned, why is nobody teaching the rewrite?*

### The story (editorial chapters)

**I. The notebook.**
The first version of the Method was a private system: index cards of harsh thoughts on one side, rebuilt sentences on the other, and a rule — no sentence gets rebuilt with a lie. It had to be *believable* or the mind spat it out. That rule survived everything that came after. It's still the heart of the Rewrite.

**II. The reading.**
We went looking for permission to hope, and found something better: mechanisms. Decades of research on cognitive reappraisal, self-distancing, expressive writing, and identity-based habit — rigorous, replicated, and almost entirely absent from the self-help shelf, where it had been replaced by louder, emptier things. The science existed. The translation didn't.

**III. The others.**
We shared the system with friends who "had it together." Every single one had a narrator. The executive whose voice said *they're going to find out.* The surgeon whose voice said *one mistake and you're done.* Competence, we learned, doesn't quiet the critic. It just gives it better material.

**IV. The build.**
We assembled the team this work deserved: clinicians to keep it honest, researchers to keep it accurate, writers to keep it human, designers to make practice feel like something you'd choose. We wrote three bans into the founding documents: no hype, no shame, no pretending this replaces therapy.

**V. The point.**
We measure success in a strange way for a subscription business: we design for graduation. The goal is that one day you notice you've stopped needing us — and that the voice you think with, finally, is yours.

## METHOD `/method`

### Hero
**Eyebrow:** THE SELV METHOD
**Headline:** A system for rewriting the voice you think with.
**Sub:** Three movements. Twelve minutes a day. Grounded in how the brain actually changes.

### The problem, precisely (sticky sequence)
**Step 1:** Your inner dialogue runs at roughly a thousand words a minute when it spirals — commentary, prediction, verdict. You don't hear most of it. You just live inside its conclusions.
**Step 2:** Most of that script was drafted early, by other people: a parent's sigh, a teacher's margin note, a room that laughed at the wrong moment. Useful once, maybe. Never updated since.
**Step 3:** You've been editing your behavior — more preparation, more achievement, more armor — while the script writes the behavior. Work upstream.

### Movement I — AUDIT
**You can't rewrite a script you've never read.**

The Audit makes the invisible voice visible. Over your first days you'll map when the narrator speaks, what it says verbatim, and which of six patterns it favors. Most people meet their dominant narrator inside four minutes — and the recognition is physical, a small cold shock of *oh, that's been running my life.*

**The six narrators:**
- **The Perfectionist** — "If it's not flawless, it's failure." Confuses standards with safety.
- **The Guard** — "Don't try, and you can't lose." Sells protection, delivers smallness.
- **The Ghost** — "Take up less space." Learned invisibility, performed as politeness.
- **The Prosecutor** — "Let's review everything you did wrong." Runs the 2 a.m. tribunal.
- **The Pleaser** — "Keep them happy and you'll be safe." Trades self for approval, daily.
- **The Prophet** — "This will go badly. It always does." Forecasts pain and calls it realism.

Naming the pattern matters more than it seems: affect labeling — putting feelings and patterns into words — measurably reduces the brain's threat response. The narrator is quieter the moment it's named.

### Movement II — REWRITE
**Not affirmations. Authorship.**

Standing at a mirror shouting "I am confident" fails for a reason: the mind rejects statements it can't verify. The Rewrite builds sentences that *survive your own cross-examination*, using three operations:

- **Distance** — shift from "I always freeze" to "I'm having the thought that I'll freeze." Self-distancing (including talking to yourself by name) reliably lowers emotional reactivity. The thought becomes weather, not fact.
- **Evidence** — put the thought on trial. "I always freeze" meets the standup question you asked last Tuesday. Not positive thinking — accurate thinking. The critic hates accuracy.
- **Author** — write the replacement in your own dialect, at a believability of 7/10 or better. Not "I am fearless" but "I've done hard rooms before. I can do this one at 80% and that's enough."

**Live demo caption:** Try it. Watch "I always freeze in meetings" become a sentence you'd actually believe.

### Movement III — REP
**A voice becomes yours the way an accent does: repetition.**

Insight fades in about seventy-two hours unless it's practiced. The Rep movement turns your rewritten sentences into a daily practice — brief structured sessions, situation rehearsals before hard moments, and the Identity Ledger, where every brave act is logged as evidence. Spaced repetition for the self. Twelve minutes a day, most days. That's the whole ask.

### The science layer (six mechanism cards — claim / mechanism / anchor)
1. **Naming emotions calms the brain.** Affect labeling dampens amygdala reactivity while engaging regulatory prefrontal regions. *(Lieberman et al., affect labeling literature.)*
2. **Reframing changes feeling, not just thought.** Cognitive reappraisal is among the best-studied emotion-regulation strategies, with reliable effects on negative affect. *(Gross; reappraisal meta-analyses.)*
3. **Talking to yourself by name creates useful distance.** Distanced self-talk reduces reactivity, even under social stress, at low cognitive cost. *(Kross et al.)*
4. **Affirming values — not traits — lowers defensiveness.** Values-based self-affirmation buffers threat and improves receptivity to hard feedback. *(Steele; Cohen & Sherman.)*
5. **Identity drives habit more than willpower does.** Habits anchored to identity statements and if-then plans outlast motivation. *(Implementation intentions — Gollwitzer; identity-based habit literature.)*
6. **The brain remains plastic.** Self-directed practice physically changes networks at every adult age. Change is slower at 50 than 15 — and entirely available.

### What it isn't
Not affirmations shouted at mirrors. Not toxic positivity — some thoughts are accurate and deserve action, not reframing. Not a personality transplant; introverts stay introverts, with a steadier voice. **Not therapy** — and we'll say so clearly whenever therapy is the right door. *(Scope note follows.)*

## INTERNAL DIALOGUE LAB `/lab/dialogue`

*(A dedicated wing of the Lab — the flagship free experience beyond the Audit.)*

**Headline:** The Internal Dialogue Lab
**Line:** Your narrator, in the workshop.

**Intro:** Everything here works on sentences — the actual words your inner voice uses. Bring the real ones. Polite paraphrases don't respond to treatment.

**Stations:**
1. **Transcription Booth.** Two minutes: write the narrator's last monologue verbatim, ugly grammar and all. You can't rewrite what you won't quote.
2. **The Attribution Desk.** For one recurring line, answer: *Whose voice is this, originally?* Most people can name the person, the room, even the year. The line loses authority when it gains an author.
3. **The Courtroom.** Evidence for, evidence against, and the verdict a fair judge would give. (Spoiler: rarely the narrator's.)
4. **The Translation Table.** Same warning, kinder dialect: "You'll embarrass yourself" becomes "This matters to you and you want to do it well." Every critic line is a bodyguard with terrible manners; translate the protection out of the threat.
5. **The Recording Studio.** Read your rewritten sentences aloud, in your own voice, thirty seconds. (Hearing yourself say it does something the page can't.) Optional, mic stays local, nothing uploads.

## COMMUNITY `/community`

**Headline:** The Commons
**Serif:** *Do the quiet work in good company.*

Self-work has a lonely reputation it doesn't deserve. The narrator does its best work in isolation — it's much less convincing in a room where six other people have the same one.

**Circles.** Six to eight members, matched by intent and timezone, one structured prompt a week. Small enough that absence is noticed kindly; structured enough that no one has to perform.
**The Forum.** Every post declares what it's seeking — *support, perspectives,* or *accountability* — so you get what you asked for, not advice you didn't. The only reaction is "I see you." There are no counts to compete over.
**Live rituals.** Open Practice, Wednesdays: twenty minutes of co-working on yourself, cameras optional. Monthly conversations with researchers and clinicians. Quarterly Threshold Stories — members telling the true version, relapses included.

## FAQ `/faq` (launch set)

**Getting started**
- **What actually is SELV?** An education and practice platform for confidence and inner dialogue: a method, daily tools, courses, programs, and a community — built on published psychology, not personality.
- **How much time does it take?** Twelve minutes a day is the designed dose. Three minutes counts on hard days. There is no falling behind; the practice waits.
- **I've read all the books. How is this different?** Books deliver insight; insight decays in about three days without practice. SELV is the practice layer — the place ideas become reps become evidence.

**The Method & science**
- **Is this scientifically supported?** The components are — reappraisal, self-distancing, affect labeling, expressive writing, implementation intentions are among the best-replicated tools in behavioral science. Our specific assembly of them is supported by our member outcomes, which we publish annually with methodology. We'll never claim more than the evidence holds.
- **Is this therapy?** No. *(Scope note, in full.)* Many members use SELV alongside therapy; many therapists like it that way. But if you're dealing with trauma, clinical anxiety or depression, or thoughts of self-harm, please start at our Resources page — the door to real help, today.
- **Do affirmations work?** Trait affirmations ("I am confident!") mostly don't — and can backfire when your mind rejects them. Values affirmation and believable rewritten self-statements do have evidence behind them. That difference is basically our whole second movement.

**Membership & billing**
- **Can I cancel easily?** Two clicks, in Settings, no chat with a "retention specialist." Your Ledger and journal remain exportable for 90 days after.
- **What's the refund policy?** Thirty days, full, no interrogation. One question — "what should we improve?" — and answering is optional.
- **Why no lifetime deal?** Because we design for graduation, not for owning you. A lifetime deal would be a bet against our own method.

**Privacy & data**
- **Can you read my journal?** No. It's end-to-end encrypted; the keys are yours. This also means we can't recover it if you lose your keys — we trade convenience for dignity, and we say so upfront.
- **What happens to my data if I leave?** Export everything (PDF/JSON) in Settings, then true deletion — not deactivation — within 30 days of request.

**Community & access**
- **Do I have to join the community?** No. It's a door, never a hallway. Fully private practice is a first-class way to use SELV.
- **Is SELV accessible?** WCAG 2.2 AAA is our working target: full keyboard support, screen-reader tested, reduced-motion parity, transcripts and captions on everything, plain-language mode. Our Accessibility Statement lists current audit results and known issues — publicly.

**Still unsure?** Write to a human → We answer within two business days, personally.
