# 00 — Content Guide

> How SELV writes. Every writer, editor, and contributor works from this page. It compresses the brand voice (`design/01-brand-identity.md §6`) into working rules for content production.

## The voice in one paragraph

Write like a mentor who has done the work: calm, precise, warm without being soft. Every claim earns its place with a mechanism ("this works because…") or an honest admission ("we don't know yet"). Speak to the person the reader is becoming, never to their fear. Short sentences carry weight; long sentences carry nuance. If a sentence could appear on a motivational poster, cut it.

## Hard rules

1. **Mechanism or cut.** Every technique must explain *why it works* in plain language, with the research tradition named (reappraisal, self-distancing, implementation intentions, exposure, self-affirmation, expressive writing). If we can't explain it, we don't publish it.
2. **Honest scope.** We are education and practice, not therapy. Every piece that touches clinical territory (anxiety, depression, trauma) carries the scope note (see below) and points to real help.
3. **No miracle timelines.** Never "in 5 minutes" or "overnight." Real ranges: "most people notice a difference in two to six weeks of daily practice."
4. **Banned words:** broken, fix (people), hack, crush, grind, unstoppable, alpha, 10x, secret, guru, manifest, vibrate, limitless, beast mode, level up (about people), "your best life."
5. **Preferred words:** build, practice, rehearse, notice, name, author, evidence, rep, draft, rewrite, steady, capable.
6. **Second person, present tense** for exercises. Third person for research. First person plural ("we") only for editorial stance.
7. **Numbers are honest.** Cite sample sizes and effect honesty ("a modest but reliable effect"). Never inflate.
8. **Every piece ends in practice.** One concrete rep, one reflection question, or one tool link. No dead ends.
9. **One idea per piece.** If an article needs three ideas, it's three articles.
10. **Read-aloud test.** If it sounds embarrassing spoken to a smart friend, rewrite it.

## The scope note (standard)

> *SELV teaches evidence-informed practices for everyday confidence and self-talk. It isn't therapy, and it isn't a substitute for it. If you're dealing with persistent depression, anxiety that disrupts your life, trauma, or thoughts of harming yourself, the bravest rep is talking to a professional. Our [Resources page](/resources) lists where to start, including free and crisis options.*

## Structure conventions

- **Articles:** 1,400–2,500 words. Structure: the felt problem (150 words, mirror not lecture) → the mechanism → the practice (numbered, doable today) → what to expect (honest timeline + common failure points) → one rep + one reflection question.
- **Lessons:** one skill each, 10–20 min, always: Learn (why) → Watch it in yourself (noticing exercise) → Do (rep) → Log (Ledger prompt).
- **Exercises:** name, time cost, mechanism tag, when to use it, numbered steps, "what usually gets in the way," variation for hard days.
- **Stories:** struggle included, relapse included, consent documented, no income or romance-outcome claims, methodology disclosure in footer.

## Reading level & accessibility

Default to grade 8–10 reading level without dumbing down ideas — shorter sentences, common words, defined terms. Every article ships with a 3-sentence "In brief" summary (used for plain mode, previews, and meta descriptions). Alt text is editorial, not mechanical.
