# 03 — The Practice Library

> The complete launch set of exercises, routines, scripts, prompts, challenges, and worksheets. Every item follows the exercise format from `00-content-guide.md`: name · time · mechanism · when to use · steps · what gets in the way · hard-day variation. These power the daily Reps, the Lab, Programs, and the "Do the rep" blocks across all content.

## A. Confidence-Building Exercises

### A1 · The Evidence Sprint
**3 min · cognitive restructuring · use when: a specific doubt is loud**

1. Write the doubt as a single sentence, in quotes. *("I'm not ready to lead this project.")*
2. Set a 3-minute timer. List every fact from your own history that complicates the doubt — not feelings, facts. Events, dates, things people said, things you finished.
3. Stop at the timer. Read the list once, aloud if you can.
4. Re-rate the doubt's believability, 0–10, before and after.

*What gets in the way:* the mind offering counter-counter-evidence. You're not required to win; you're required to complete the list. *Hard-day variation:* find one fact. One is a sprint.

### A2 · The Smallest Brave Thing
**1–5 min · graded exposure · use when: avoidance is running the day**

1. Name the thing you're avoiding.
2. Ask: what's the smallest version of this I could do in the next hour? (Not the task — the smallest *slice*: opening the document, sending "can we find 15 minutes?", asking one question in the meeting.)
3. Shrink it until your honest answer to "could I do this today?" is "obviously."
4. Do it. Log it in the Ledger with the word "despite."

*What gets in the way:* contempt for the small step ("this is nothing"). The step isn't the point; the identity entry is. *Hard-day variation:* the smallest brave thing can be telling one person the truth about how the day is going.

### A3 · The Compliment Vault Deposit
**2 min · positive data logging · use when: praise just landed (or is about to be forgotten)**

1. Write the compliment verbatim, who said it, and the context.
2. Add one line: what did I actually do that produced this?
3. File it. Do not edit it down. Do not add "but."

*What gets in the way:* the deflection reflex ("they were being nice"). Log the deflection too — it's data about your accountant. *Use the vault:* re-read three entries before any high-stakes moment.

### A4 · Borrowed Eyes
**5 min · perspective-taking · use when: you can't see your own competence**

1. Pick someone who respects you — real, specific.
2. Write, in their voice, three sentences describing how you handled the last month. Their dialect, their generosity.
3. Underline the one claim you resist most. Ask: what do they know that I keep dismissing?

### A5 · The 80% Standard
**2 min, pre-performance · standard-setting · use when: perfectionism is inflating the stakes**

1. Before the event, define — in writing — what an 80% version looks like. Concretely: "I cover the three main points, I answer questions honestly, I close on time."
2. Declare it the pass line: *"Eighty is a win today."*
3. Afterward, grade against the written standard — not the imagined perfect version.

*Why it works:* perfectionist standards are secretly undefined, which means nothing ever meets them. A written 80% is meetable, and met standards build the mastery evidence that fuels real confidence.

## B. Internal Dialogue Transformation Exercises

### B1 · The Verbatim Transcript
**2 min, as-it-happens · affect labeling · the foundation exercise**

When the narrator speaks, write the line exactly — grammar, cruelty, and all. Rules: quotes around it, no paraphrase, no rebuttal yet. Collect for one week before doing anything else. You're building the case file.

### B2 · The Attribution Desk
**10 min, weekly · source monitoring / defusion**

For each recurring line in your transcript: *Whose voice is this, originally? When did I first hear it? What was it protecting me from, then?* Write the answers as a caption under the quote, like a museum label: *"'Don't get your hopes up.' — Acquired ~1999, kitchen table. Original function: pre-blunting disappointment."* A line with a museum label never sounds quite the same again.

### B3 · The Courtroom
**10 min · cognitive restructuring (thought record)**

Columns: The charge (verbatim thought) · Evidence for (facts only) · Evidence against (facts only) · What a fair judge would say (one sentence, believability 7+) · Sentence commuted to (the working rewrite).
*What gets in the way:* putting feelings in the evidence columns ("I felt everyone was bored" is not evidence; "two people asked follow-up questions" is).

### B4 · The Translation Table
**5 min · reappraisal via function-finding**

Take one critic line. Answer: (1) What is it warning me about? (2) What does the warning reveal I care about? (3) Rewrite the warning as a kind, competent advisor would deliver it. Keep the caring, lose the cruelty.
*Example:* "You'll embarrass yourself" → cares about: doing well in front of people who matter → translation: "This matters to you. Prepare once, properly, and then trust it."

### B5 · The Second Draft (daily rewrite rep)
**4 min, daily · the core Rep**

1. Today's harshest line, verbatim.
2. Distance it: "I'm having the thought that…" (read it once in this frame).
3. Rewrite at believability 7+, in your dialect, anchored in one fact.
4. Read the rewrite aloud. Log both drafts in the Ledger — the pair is the progress.

### B6 · Name-Switch Drill
**1 min, situational · distanced self-talk**

One exhale. Then coach yourself by name, second person, three sentences max, the tone you'd use for a capable friend. Scripted in advance for known thresholds (see Article 5).

## C. Limiting Belief Exercises

### C1 · The Belief Archaeology Dig
**20 min, once per belief · schema examination**

1. State the belief plainly. *("People like me don't get to lead.")*
2. Excavate: earliest memory where this felt true? Who modeled or said it? What did believing it *protect* you from at the time?
3. Date it: roughly what year was this belief installed? Write the year next to it.
4. Audit it against your current life: list three ways your situation differs from the installation context.
5. Rewrite it as a dated artifact: *"In 2003, in that house, this was adaptive. It is now costing me X. I'm replacing it with: …"* (believability rule applies).

### C2 · The Cost Ledger
**10 min · motivational leverage**

For one limiting belief, two columns: what it has cost me (opportunities not taken, words not said, years) · what it has protected me from (be honest — beliefs persist because they pay). Then the deciding question, in writing: *Is the protection still worth the price?* Most rewrites fail without this step, because half the mind is still collecting the protection.

### C3 · The Exception Hunt
**7 min · disconfirmation**

Beliefs survive by deleting exceptions. Hunt them: find three moments — however small — when the belief's prediction failed. ("I froze"—except the time you didn't. Find it, date it, describe it in two sentences.) Exceptions are the crack where the new belief gets planted.

### C4 · Acting Ahead of the Belief
**1 week · behavioral experiment**

Choose one action the *new* belief would take this week (the old one wouldn't). Predict, in writing, what will happen (the old belief's forecast). Do the action. Record what actually happened. Compare forecasts to outcomes — you're not arguing with the belief anymore; you're letting reality out-argue it. One experiment per week; the case file builds itself.

## D. Emotional Regulation Exercises

### D1 · Name It to Tame It (precision labeling)
**90 seconds · affect labeling**

1. Stop. One breath.
2. Name the emotion — then refuse the first word and go two levels more precise. Not "stressed" → "overloaded" → "afraid of disappointing Sam specifically."
3. Say the precise sentence: "I'm feeling ___ because ___."
The precision is the mechanism: vague arousal is threatening; named, specific feeling is information.

### D2 · The Physiological Sigh
**30 seconds · autonomic downshift · use when: body is louder than mind**

Two inhales through the nose — one long, one short top-up — then one long, slow exhale through the mouth. Repeat three times. This is the fastest reliable downshift we know of; use it *before* any cognitive tool, because reasoning arrives only after arousal drops.

### D3 · The 90-Second Wave
**2 min · urge/emotion surfing**

Set a timer for 90 seconds. Feel the emotion without acting on it or arguing with it — track it like weather: where is it in the body, is it rising or falling? The insight this installs through experience: emotional surges crest and fall on their own, usually faster than the mind predicts. You are not required to do anything with a wave except let it be one.

### D4 · Weather Report (daily check-in)
**1 min, 2× daily**

Three lines: Inner weather (one metaphor: "low fog," "clear, windy") · Body location ("chest, shoulders") · What it needs ("food, honestly" / "to send the email"). Feeds the Mood Tracker; builds the interoception that all regulation depends on.

### D5 · The Container (for feelings that arrive at the wrong time)
**3 min · scheduled processing**

Write the feeling and its trigger on paper: *"Anger at the review comment. Real. Not now."* Assign it an appointment: "Tonight, 6:30, ten minutes." Keep the appointment — this is the whole trick; containers only work if the contents are actually processed. Deferral with a kept appointment is regulation; deferral without one is suppression.

## E. Visualization Techniques

### E1 · Process Rehearsal (not outcome fantasy)
**6 min · mental rehearsal / implementation intentions**

Research is clear on this distinction: visualizing the *outcome* (the applause) can reduce drive; visualizing the *process* improves performance. So:
1. Choose the event. Close your eyes, one sigh (D2).
2. Walk it in first person, real speed, ordinary details: the door, the chairs, your opening sentence in your actual voice.
3. Include one wobble — a hard question, a blank moment — and rehearse your recovery move (pause, breath, "good question, let me think for a second").
4. End at the *middle* of the event going adequately, not at triumph. You're training familiarity, not fantasy.

### E2 · The Future Self Consultation
**10 min, weekly · temporal self-continuity**

Sit with the version of you five years on, who practiced. Ask them three questions and write their answers with your pen: *What did you stop doing? What mattered less than I think? What did you start that changed things?* Instruction: their voice is calm and slightly amused. It knows things worked out more often than not.

### E3 · The Gallery of Evidence
**4 min · memory-based efficacy priming · use before hard moments**

Eyes closed. Walk a corridor hung with three specific memories of you handling something hard — real ones, chosen in advance (this exercise is prepared once, used often). Stand in front of each for three breaths. Reinhabit the body of the person who did that. Exit through the door to today's task.

## F. Meditation Scripts (for audio recording; ~pace 100 wpm, pauses marked ·)

### F1 · "The Observer" — 8 minutes, for meeting thoughts without obeying them

> Settle where you are. You don't need a special posture — just one that lets you stay. · Let the eyes close, or rest low. · Take one slow breath in through the nose… and let it leave without pushing. · Again. ·
>
> For the next few minutes, your job is unusual: you have nothing to improve. You're here to watch. ·
>
> Imagine you're sitting a few steps back from your own mind — a quiet room, one chair, and in front of you, a window where thoughts pass. · You don't open the window. You don't chase what passes. You watch, from the chair. ·
>
> A thought will come. It may be a plan. A worry. A sentence about how you're doing at this. · When it comes, name it softly, like noting a bird: *planning. · worrying. · judging.* · And let it keep moving. It was already moving. ·
>
> Some thoughts are loud. They arrive dressed as emergencies. · Name them too: *urgent-feeling.* · Notice: naming it, you stayed in the chair. The window held. ·
>
> If you find you've climbed through the window — you're three scenes into a story — that's not failure, that's the exercise. The noticing *is* the rep. Walk back to the chair. Sit. Watch. ·
>
> One more minute of watching. Nothing to improve. ·
>
> Before you finish: notice that all of this — every thought that passed — was watched by something. That watching part of you doesn't panic. It never has. It's where you're sitting right now. · You can come back to this chair in one breath, anywhere, today. ·
>
> Take one deeper breath. · Let the room return. · Open your eyes when you're ready. The window keeps playing. You know where the chair is.

### F2 · "Before the Threshold" — 5 minutes, pre-performance

> One minute from now, you'll feel steadier. Not because the nerves will vanish — because you'll have sorted them. ·
>
> Long breath in — short top-up — and a slow, full exhale. · Twice more, your pace. ·
>
> The body is loud right now. Heart quicker, chest tight. Here's the sorting: this is *arousal*, not verdict. The body doesn't know the difference between about-to-present and about-to-race. It's fueling you. Let it be fuel. ·
>
> Say inwardly, with your own name: *[Name], this matters to you. That's why it's loud.* ·
>
> Now — three facts. First: name one time you did something like this and came through. See it for a breath. · Second: name what you actually know about today's material. You know it. · Third: name the 80% version — what "good enough, truly" looks like. That's the target. Not the imaginary perfect one. ·
>
> Your opening line. Hear yourself say it, ordinary voice, once. · That's the only door you need. Everything after it follows. ·
>
> Last breath — in… · and out, longer. · Shoulders down an inch. Jaw soft. ·
>
> Go do the middle-sized brave thing. The room is lucky to have someone who prepared.

### F3 · "The Kind Audit" — 10 minutes, evening (pairs with the Adjournment Protocol)

> *(Opening settle, 4 breaths.)* · The day is over. It doesn't need re-running at full volume — it needs a fair reading, once, by someone kind. That's you, now. ·
>
> Bring up the day like pages. Don't grip. Let three moments surface on their own — whatever comes. · For each: name it, one word. · No sentences yet. Sentences at night become verdicts. ·
>
> Now the fair reading, one moment at a time: *What happened — just the footage, no commentary.* · *What it needed from you — and what you had available today.* Not what you'd have on a perfect day. Today. · Notice — most shortfalls are gaps between what was needed and what was available. That's arithmetic, not character. ·
>
> One moment from today deserved more credit than it got. Find it. Small is fine — the email sent, the tone kept, the rest taken. · Give it its line: *that was me, doing that.* ·
>
> Anything unresolved — feel it asking to be carried to bed. Set it down here instead: tomorrow has a docket, and it's on it. · Court is adjourned. ·
>
> *(Closing, slower:)* You met this day with what you had. Tomorrow you'll have what tomorrow brings. · The reading is done. The kind auditor signs the page. · Sleep is allowed now.

## G. Morning Routine — "The First Draft" (12 minutes, the flagship daily rep)

**Minute 0–2 · Arrive.** Before the phone (this is the only hard rule). One glass of water. Three physiological sighs at the window or door — light in the eyes, if there is any.
**Minute 2–4 · Weather report.** One line: inner weather, body, need (D4). Honest, not aspirational.
**Minute 4–8 · The draft.** Today's identity sentence, written fresh (not copied): *"Today I'm someone who ___."* Small and true beats grand and hollow — "…asks the question anyway" is a great day's draft. Then the day's one **threshold**: the moment most likely to summon the narrator. Write its Name-Switch script (B6), three lines.
**Minute 8–11 · One rep.** The day's assigned exercise (rotates: Second Draft / Evidence Sprint / rehearsal for today's threshold).
**Minute 11–12 · Close.** Read the draft once, aloud. "That's the plan. Done is done." The notebook closes; the day opens.

*Hard-day variation (3 minutes):* water, one sigh, weather report, and yesterday's identity sentence re-read. That counts. It fully counts.

## H. Evening Routine — "The Adjournment" (10 minutes)

The four-step protocol from Article 3, plus the Ledger deposit:
1. **Docket (2 min):** the day's charges, verbatim, fast.
2. **One fact per charge (3 min):** observable facts against each.
3. **Extract or dismiss (2 min):** one action to tomorrow's list, or the written word *dismissed*.
4. **Deposit (2 min):** one "because" sentence — a thing you did and why it worked — into the Ledger. On brutal days the deposit is: *"Showed up to this page. Because I said I would."*
5. **Adjourn (1 min):** the unremarked-fine thing, and the closing line: *Court is adjourned.*

## I. Guided Journaling Prompts (rotating library)

**For meeting the narrator:** What did the voice say today that I'd never let anyone say to a friend? · Which of its lines is oldest? How old was I? · If the critic wrote me a letter explaining what it's afraid of, what would it say?
**For evidence:** What did I do this month that past-me would not have believed? · What compliment have I never fully accepted, and what would accepting it change? · Where am I demanding proof from myself that I'd never demand from someone I love?
**For identity:** Complete honestly: "I'm the kind of person who…" — then mark each answer *inherited* or *chosen*. · What am I doing when I like who I'm being? · Which role am I still performing that expired years ago?
**For hard days:** What is this feeling asking for — and what is it settling for? · What would "enough for today" look like if I were allowed to define it? · Who could I tell the true version of today to?
**For direction:** What do I want that I keep translating into something more acceptable? · What would I attempt this year if the narrator had to stay silent for it? · What's the smallest version of that, dated this week?

## J. Daily Challenges (30-day "Small Brave Things" deck — sample 15)

1. Ask one question in a meeting within the first ten minutes. 2. Give one specific compliment out loud. 3. Say "I don't know" once, without apologizing for it. 4. State a preference where you'd usually say "either's fine." 5. Send the message you've been drafting in your head for a week. 6. Wear/keep visible the thing you like but feel "isn't you." 7. Let a silence sit for three seconds before filling it. 8. Correct someone who got your name/details wrong, kindly. 9. Ask for a small, reasonable thing you'd normally self-deny ("Could we move this to 2?"). 10. Share one opinion before hearing the room's. 11. Decline one thing without a three-line excuse. "I can't take that on" is complete. 12. Tell someone what you're working on becoming. Out loud. 13. Eat/sit/walk somewhere alone, un-phoned, ten minutes. 14. Ask a follow-up question instead of performing understanding. 15. Log the day's brave thing — then tell one person you did it.

*Deck rules:* one card per day; any card may be swapped for "smaller version of the same"; every completion is a Ledger entry with the word "despite."

## K. Worksheets (print/PDF set)

1. **The Dialogue Audit Sheet** — 7-day transcript grid: trigger · verbatim line · narrator archetype guess · body signal. Feeds the Audit and B1/B2.
2. **The Courtroom Record** — B3 as a fillable page: charge / evidence for / against / fair judge / commuted sentence, with a believability dial (0–10) to mark before and after.
3. **The Belief Archaeology Card** — one belief per card: belief · installed (year, place, source) · original protection · current cost · exceptions found · replacement draft · this week's behavioral experiment (C1–C4 on one page).
4. **The Working Sentences Card** — wallet/lockscreen format: three believability-7 sentences + one interrogative + the Name-Switch script. "The card you read before the door."
5. **The Kept Promises Ledger** — a month grid where the only tracked metric is *promises to self, kept*, with a weekly "because" reflection row.
6. **The 80% Standard Sheet** — event · the written 80% definition · post-event grade against the written line · what the imagined-perfect version was demanding.
7. **The Fear Ladder Builder** — goal at top, eight rungs of graded exposure below, each with: step · predicted discomfort (0–10) · actual (filled after) · what I learned. (The prediction-vs-actual gap column is the confidence engine — predictions run reliably high.)

## L. Checklists

**Before a high-stakes moment:** Working Sentences card read · one Gallery walk (E3) or process rehearsal (E1) done this week · 80% standard written · physiological sighs ×3 at the door · opening line rehearsed once, out loud.
**After (win):** booked at market value with a "because" sentence · deflection stamps caught and named · one person told, plainly, without the discount.
**After (stumble):** footage vs. commentary separated · one fact per charge · one extractable action or *dismissed* · kind-audit line: what was available today · the stumble logged as a rep, because showing up was one.
**Weekly review (Sundays, 15 min):** Ledger read-through (out loud, at least five entries) · practice-week count (honest, no drama either way) · one exception found to an old belief · next week's single threshold chosen · one card drawn from the challenge deck for Monday.
