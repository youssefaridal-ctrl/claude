## Success Story 1 — Maya, 34, product lead
### "The sentence I rehearsed four times"

**Before.** There was a meeting every Thursday, and a version of me who spent Wednesday night preparing to be adequate in it. I'd draft a comment, edit it, rank it against what smarter people might say, and usually file it under *not worth the risk*. My performance reviews said "more executive presence." My inner voice translated: *they can tell.*

The line that ran my life — I know its exact wording now, which is a strange kind of intimacy — was: *"If you say it and it's obvious, they'll wonder why you're here."* I thought it was insight. It was a script.

**The turn.** I took the Audit on a Tuesday at 1 a.m., expecting a horoscope. What I got was a name — the Perfectionist, with a secondary Ghost pattern — and one sentence in the results that I screenshotted: *"You don't have a speaking problem. You have a pre-trial problem: every sentence faces prosecution before it's allowed out."* I paid for the year that night, which is exactly the kind of impulse purchase I never make.

**The practice.** The unglamorous truth: it was transcripts first. Two weeks of writing down what the voice actually said, verbatim, which was embarrassing and then clarifying — it had *five lines*. Five. I'd mistaken a broken record for an oracle.

My first working sentence failed. "My perspective is valuable" — believability 4, the fact-checker threw it out instantly. The one that held was smaller: *"I've never once asked a question that ended a career. Ask the question."* Believability 8. I know how unimpressive that sounds. It held on a Thursday, which is worth more than impressive.

**The relapse.** Week six, a director cut me off mid-sentence, and the old narrator came back with reinforcements. I skipped practice for nine days — the app's "the chair is still there" note eventually got me to do a three-minute rep, mostly out of politeness. Restarting turned out to be the actual skill. Nobody tells you that. The Ledger's counter-evidence search — I typed "I can't speak up" and it showed me eleven entries of me doing exactly that — did what no pep talk has ever done. You can't argue with your own handwriting.

**Now** (eight months): I rehearse a sentence once, out of respect for the room, not fear of it. I still meet the Perfectionist most Thursdays. I recognize her now — she's the security guard from a school I graduated from years ago. I let her check my badge, and then I go in.

## Success Story 2 — David, 47, operations director
### "Rebuilding without the blueprint"

**Before.** The divorce paperwork listed the assets, and I remember thinking: there's no line item for the part of me that just left. I'd been "husband and provider" for nineteen years. Take that away and what's left was — a man who was very good at logistics and slept three hours a night reviewing two decades of choices at high volume. My narrator wasn't cruel like people describe. Mine was a Prophet, and it was calm: *the good part is over now.* Delivered like a weather report. That's what made it convincing.

**The turn.** My sister sent me the essay about the 2 a.m. tribunal with the message "this is you." I did the evening protocol from the article for a week — just the notebook, no account — because it was free and I was tired. The night sessions got shorter. That earned my attention: I run operations; I respect things that work.

**The practice.** I joined for the Rebuild roadmap. The inventory weeks undid me a little, in a good way — listing what survived the wreck. It turned out to be more than furniture: steadiness, twenty years of solved problems, two kids who tell me the truth. The Belief Archaeology on "the good part is over" found its author, by the way. It wasn't mine. It was my father's sentence about *his* life, installed at our kitchen table around 1989. I wrote the museum label. I actually cried, which the worksheet does not have a field for.

**The relapse.** First holidays alone, I stopped everything for three weeks and the Prophet ran unopposed. What got me back wasn't willpower — it was my Circle. Six people, and one of them just wrote "chair's still there, David" in the weekly thread. That week I learned the difference between being watched and being witnessed.

**Now** (fourteen months): My identity sentences are still provisional — the roadmap says that's the design, and I've stopped needing them to be final. Last month my daughter asked what I was always writing in the mornings. I told her: "Evidence." She said "of what?" and I said "that the good part isn't over." The Prophet had no comment.

## Case Study (course-embedded) — "The believability failure"
*Used in The Rewrite, Deep — Lesson 4. A teaching case, 400 words, anonymized pattern.*

A member — call her R. — arrives at week 3 with a rewrite that isn't taking: *"I am a confident and capable leader."* Ratings show the problem immediately: believability 3, and her transcript shows the fact-checker's rebuttals arriving within seconds of each repetition ("then why did the Q3 launch slip?"). Classic trait-affirmation rejection: each rep is rehearsing the prosecution.

The intervention is not encouragement — it's *shrinkage*. Working the Believability Dial: "I am a confident leader" (3) → "I'm becoming a more confident leader" (5, still rejected: 'becoming' reads as spin to her) → "I ran the Q3 post-mortem without deflecting blame, and the team stayed" (8). The sentence that finally holds is barely an affirmation at all. It's a fact with a spine.

Teaching points: (1) rewrites fail *downward* — the fix is almost always smaller, never grander; (2) the member's own dialect matters — R. is an engineer, and her holding sentence sounds like an incident report, which is exactly why it holds; (3) track the dial over weeks: by week 9, R.'s original sentence — unchanged — rates 6. The sentence didn't move. The evidence under it did.

## Podcast — "The Inner Voice" (show bible + launch episodes)

**Format:** 40–55 min conversations + 10-min solo "Field Notes" episodes. Tone: curious, unhurried, zero hype. Every episode ends with the same two questions to guests: *"What does your inner voice say these days — verbatim?"* and *"What's the smallest rep you still do?"*

**Launch season (8 episodes):**
1. **The Voice Nobody Hears But You** — a cognitive scientist on where inner speech comes from, why some people have none, and what it's *for*. (Solo intro to the season's premise.)
2. **The Impostor Files** — a decorated surgeon on twenty years of "one mistake and you're done," and the audit that changed her relationship to it.
3. **What Therapists Wish Self-Help Knew** — a clinical psychologist on the line between practice and therapy, drawn honestly. (Our scope note, as an episode.)
4. **The Confidence of Quiet People** — an introverted CEO on presence without performance; the Visibility Ladder in the wild.
5. **Field Notes: The 2 a.m. Tribunal** — solo, 12 min: the adjournment protocol, narrated for night listening.
6. **Rebuilt: A Story in Three Drafts** — a member (real, consented) on identity after rupture; long-form version of the Rebuild roadmap.
7. **Your Brain on Reappraisal** — a neuroscientist on what actually changes when you rewrite a thought — and what doesn't. Honest about effect sizes.
8. **The Graduation Episode** — why we design for members leaving; a conversation about endings with a hospice-turned-leadership coach. (The brand thesis as a season finale.)

## Video Content (scripts/briefs)

**V1 — The Method film (2:30, site hero for /method).** No faces speaking to camera. Hands, notebooks, doorways, mornings — the photography direction in motion. VO reads a compressed manifesto over the three movements demonstrated visually: a transcript being written; a sentence struck through and rewritten; a person at a threshold, entering. Ends on the line: *"You are the author now."* No music swell; room tone and a low piano figure.

**V2 — "One Rep" series (60–90 s each, social/library).** One exercise per film, demonstrated in real time with on-screen text — the Evidence Sprint actually timed; the physiological sigh actually breathed. No presenter charisma; the exercise is the star. 12 films at launch, one per core exercise.

**V3 — Story films (3–4 min, per Success Story).** Documentary grade per the photography direction: real locations, imperfect audio embraced, the relapse chapter mandatory in the edit. Title cards use the subject's verbatim before/after sentences.

**V4 — Course lesson format.** Talking-head kept to <30% of runtime; the rest is typography, blueprint animation, and worked examples on screen. Every lesson video ends with the rep assignment as a full-screen card, read aloud.
