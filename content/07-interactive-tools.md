# 07 — Interactive Tools: Complete Content

> Full question sets, scales, scoring logic, and result copy for the platform's eleven interactive tools. UX flows per `design/03-site-architecture.md` (T4 template). **Standard header on every assessment:** *"This is a self-reflection tool, not a diagnosis. Honest answers beat impressive ones."* All scoring is transparent — members can always see how their result was computed (trust rule: no black boxes about the self).

---

## 1. Confidence Assessment ("The Confidence Map")

**Format:** 24 items · 5-point scale (Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Always) · ~6 min · scores six dimensions (4 items each, ★ = reverse-scored).

**Dimension 1 — Voice** (speaking, visibility): 1. In groups, I say what I think before checking the room's temperature. · 2. When I disagree, the disagreement makes it out of my head. · 3. ★ I rehearse sentences several times before saying them in meetings. · 4. I ask questions when I don't understand, even in front of people I respect.
**Dimension 2 — Worth** (self-appraisal): 5. When something goes well, I can name my part in it. · 6. ★ Compliments make me immediately explain why they're overstated. · 7. I'd describe my standards for myself as high but meetable. · 8. ★ When I succeed, my first explanation is luck or timing.
**Dimension 3 — Boundaries:** 9. I can decline a request without manufacturing an excuse. · 10. ★ I say yes to things I resent doing, to keep the peace. · 11. People close to me know what I won't accept. · 12. I ask for what I need before I'm desperate.
**Dimension 4 — Recovery** (repair after setbacks): 13. After an embarrassing moment, I'm functional again within hours, not weeks. · 14. ★ I replay social mistakes for days. · 15. One bad outcome stays one bad outcome — it doesn't become a verdict on me. · 16. I can laugh at myself without it curdling into self-attack.
**Dimension 5 — Action under doubt:** 17. I can start things before I feel ready. · 18. ★ I wait for certainty before acting, and certainty rarely comes. · 19. Nervousness and I can share a room while I do the thing anyway. · 20. I volunteer for stretches slightly beyond my current evidence.
**Dimension 6 — Self-trust** (kept promises): 21. When I tell myself I'll do something, I mostly believe me. · 22. ★ My plans for myself are where my reliability goes to die. · 23. I keep small promises to myself even when nobody would notice. · 24. Past evidence tells me I generally figure things out.

**Scoring:** dimension score = ((mean − 1) ÷ 4) × 100, a true 0–100 scale. No global "confidence score" is shown as a single number — the *shape* is the result (radar chart), because a single number invites verdicts and the shape invites work.
**Result copy pattern (per dimension, 3 bands):** e.g., Voice 0–40: "Your thoughts are clearing a high bar before they're allowed out loud. That's not shyness — it's a pre-trial system, and it's negotiable. Start here: the Visibility Ladder, rung one." · 41–70: "Your voice shows up when the stakes are low and goes quiet when they rise. The gap is situational, which is good news — situations can be rehearsed." · 71–100: "Voice is a strength — your work likely lives in another dimension. Lend this one's evidence to the others."
**Retake cadence:** quarterly (feeds the Quarterly Delta overlay).

## 2. Mindset Assessment ("The Operating Assumptions Audit")

**Format:** 16 items (4 per axis) · agree–disagree 5-point · ~5 min · maps four assumption pairs (not "fixed vs. growth" alone — richer, and honest about nuance).

**Axis 1 — Malleability** (Can people like me change?): "People's core character is set by adulthood." ★ · "With practice, I've changed things about myself that felt permanent." · "Confidence is something you're born with or not." ★ · "Skills I lack are mostly skills I haven't built yet." (+1)
**Axis 2 — Attribution** (Where do outcomes come from?): "When things go wrong for me, it usually traces to something about me." ★ · "My wins usually have my fingerprints on them somewhere." · "Luck explains more of my success than effort does." ★ · "I can usually name the specific actions that produced my results." (+1)
**Axis 3 — Permission** (Who is allowed to act?): "I need more preparation than most people before I've earned the right to speak." ★ · "Being a beginner in public is acceptable to me." · "People like me don't usually get to do things like that." ★ · "I can want things openly." (+1)
**Axis 4 — Failure meaning:** "A failure reveals what I am." ★ · "A failure is data about an attempt." · "If I might do it badly, better not to do it at all." ★ · "I expect first attempts to be rough drafts." (+1)

**Results:** four sliders, each with a named position and one paragraph. E.g., Axis 4 low: "Failure is currently filed under *identity* in your system — every stumble amends the permanent record. The reframe that moves this isn't optimism; it's ontology: attempts are drafts, and drafts are supposed to be rough. Your exercise: the prediction-vs-actual column of the Fear Ladder, where your forecasts get an accuracy score." Each result links its matching course.

## 3. Internal Dialogue Analyzer (the Audit, `/lab/audit` — flagship)

**Format:** 12 items · 5-point · 4 min · returns dominant + secondary Narrator, six-dimension radar, three personalized insights. *(The conversion-critical free tool; results shown before any email ask.)*

**Items (each maps to two narrators, weighted):**
1. When something goes well, my first thought is that I got lucky. *(Prophet +2, Perfectionist +1)*
2. Before I speak in a group, I test the sentence for stupidity. *(Perfectionist +2, Ghost +1)*
3. After social events, I run a highlight reel of my mistakes. *(Prosecutor +2)*
4. I downplay wants so I won't be disappointed. *(Guard +2, Prophet +1)*
5. I feel responsible for the moods of people around me. *(Pleaser +2)*
6. Taking up space — time, attention, airtime — feels like a debt I'll owe. *(Ghost +2, Pleaser +1)*
7. When I imagine trying something new, I picture how it fails first. *(Prophet +2, Guard +1)*
8. "Good enough" feels like a euphemism for failure. *(Perfectionist +2)*
9. I'd rather not try than try and be seen struggling. *(Guard +2, Ghost +1)*
10. Old mistakes still get called as witnesses against me, years later. *(Prosecutor +2)*
11. I agree to things mid-sentence and regret them by the end of the sentence. *(Pleaser +2)*
12. Compliments feel like errors that will be corrected soon. *(Prosecutor +1, Prophet +1, Ghost +1)*

**Scoring:** narrator totals normalized; dominant = highest (ties broken by items 8/9/6/3/11/7 as tiebreak markers per archetype). Radar dimensions: harshness, forecasting, visibility-comfort, approval-dependence, evidence-acceptance, self-permission (computed from item groupings).

**Result copy (per narrator — structure: recognition ¶ · origin ¶ · the work ¶ · first rep). Example, The Perfectionist:**
> **Your dominant narrator: The Perfectionist.**
> Its core sentence: *"If it's not flawless, it's failure."* You know this voice as "standards." It runs pre-trial reviews on your sentences, post-mortems on your performances, and a permanent gap between what you did and what would have been enough — a gap it never defines, which is how it never closes.
> It was almost certainly adaptive once. Somewhere, precision was safety: praise arrived for flawless, or criticism arrived for less. The narrator learned the equation and never unlearned it.
> The work is not lowering your standards — it's *writing them down.* Undefined standards are unfalsifiable; written ones are meetable, and met standards are how evidence gets in.
> **Your first rep:** the 80% Standard exercise, before your next real task. Five minutes. Define — in writing — what "good enough, truly" looks like. Then watch what the narrator does to the written line. That reaction is your curriculum.

*(Equivalent copy exists for all six narrators; secondary narrator gets a two-line note: "Your supporting cast: The Ghost — the Perfectionist decides the bar, the Ghost decides you shouldn't be seen attempting it. They work shifts.")*

## 4. Mood Tracker ("Inner Weather")

**Capture (30 s, up to 2×/day):** (1) Weather metaphor picker — 8 states: Clear · Bright, breezy · Overcast · Low fog · Charged (storm coming) · Raining · Cold snap · After-rain (clearing) — metaphors, not scores, because "how anxious, 1–10?" invites the Prosecutor. (2) Body location chips (head/jaw/chest/stomach/shoulders/everywhere/can't tell). (3) Optional one-line note. (4) Optional need chip (rest/food/movement/people/quiet/to say something).
**Insights (weekly+):** patterns by day/time ("Low fog clusters on Sunday evenings"), lead indicators ("Charged often precedes the Thursday meeting — rehearsal rep suggested?"), and the honest caveat baked into copy: "Two weeks of data is weather. Patterns need a month. Verdicts need never."
**Safety:** persistent low states across 14+ days trigger a gentle, non-alarmist check-in card with the scope note and Resources link — written by clinicians, tested to not feel like surveillance.

## 5. Habit Tracker (member instrument — content layer)

**Add-habit flow copy (identity-first, enforced):** Step 1: "Who does this habit belong to? Finish the sentence: *I'm someone who…*" Step 2: "Now the habit — the 2-minute version first. What's the smallest real rep?" Step 3: "Wire it: After I [existing cue], I will [rep]." Step 4: "Which days count as a kept week? (Default: 4 of 7 — grace is architecture, not weakness.)"
**State copy:** kept week: "Week kept. That's a promise with your name on it." · missed day: (no message — silence is the design) · lapsed 5+ days: "The chair is still there. Three minutes counts." · resumed: "Resumed. The restart is the skill."

## 6. Goal Planner ("Seasons") — content layer

**Structure copy:** Identity statements (max 3): "Not goals — directions. *I'm becoming someone who…*" · Season (12 weeks): "One theme. Two or three goals. The limit is the feature — every yes to a fourth goal is a no to the first three." · Moves (weekly): "Verbs, calendar-sized: *Draft the outline Tuesday.* If a move needs a better week, it's too big — shrink it."
**Season review prompts:** What did this season prove? · What did it cost, honestly? · What does the evidence (Ledger auto-count shown) say versus what the narrator says? · Next season's theme, in one word?

## 7. Vision Board ("The Draft Board")

Reframed from fantasy collage to **identity drafting**: three columns — **Becoming** (identity sentences, max 5, each requiring one *current* evidence line: drafts must touch ground) · **Building** (this season's goals, imported from Planner) · **Keeping** (what stays: values, people, practices — the anti-makeover column; who you are isn't a teardown).
Media: text-first with optional images; every image requires a caption answering "what does this *feel like* on an ordinary Tuesday?" — the prompt that converts fantasy into direction. Board review is a quarterly ritual paired with the Delta.

## 8. Fear Challenge Generator ("The Deck")

**Input:** domain (voice at work / social / asking / visibility / solitude) + current comfort (1–3). **Output:** one card from the graded deck (`03 §J` plus 45 more, tiered), with: the challenge · why this size ("big enough to count, small enough to actually happen") · a Name-Switch script slot · prediction field ("What does the narrator forecast? We'll check its accuracy after.") · post-completion capture (actual vs. predicted discomfort + one-line Ledger entry).
**Reroll rules copy:** "Swap for a smaller version anytime — doors, not gates. You can't swap *up* twice in a row; ambition is also how the Perfectionist sabotages."

## 9. Life Wheel ("The Foundation Survey")

Eight spokes, renamed to load-bearing language: Body · People · Craft (work as skill, not status) · Voice (being known truly) · Play · Meaning · Order (money/logistics) · Inner weather. Two ratings per spoke — *satisfaction* (0–10) and *attention lately* (0–10) — because the gap between the two is the actual finding ("Craft: satisfied 8, attended 9. People: satisfied 4, attended 2 — the wheel isn't unbalanced; the attention is.").
**Result copy rule:** never "your life is unbalanced" (verdict); always "one spoke is asking for one season of attention" (direction) → hands off to the Planner with a suggested theme.

## 10. Values Assessment ("The Load-Bearing Walls")

**Format:** forced-choice tournament (24 value cards → pairwise choices → top 5 → final ranking of 3) — forced choice because rating everything "important" is what everyone does and it produces nothing.
**Card set (24):** honesty · craft · loyalty · freedom · learning · comfort · courage · beauty · justice · belonging · achievement · calm · generosity · independence · humor · depth · adventure · security · recognition · service · family · health · creation · faith/meaning.
**Post-ranking prompts (the actual assessment):** For each of your top 3 — "When did you last *spend* something on this value (time, money, comfort, approval)? A value you never pay for is a decoration." · "Where does your calendar contradict this ranking?" · "Which current struggle is actually two of your values arguing?" (Card pairs flagged automatically: freedom×security, belonging×independence, achievement×calm.)
**Output:** the three walls, each with the user's own evidence line + one "this week's expenditure" commitment → Ledger. Values feed the Rewrite (values-affirmation exercises reference them by name).

## 11. Daily Reflection Tool ("The Adjournment," in-app)

The evening protocol (`03 §H`) as a 4-screen guided flow: **Docket** (fast-entry list, one charge per line) → **Facts** (each charge resurfaces with the prompt "One observable fact:") → **Extract or dismiss** (per charge: [→ tomorrow's list] or a satisfying *DISMISSED* stamp interaction — the stamp is the UI's one indulgence) → **Deposit & adjourn** ("One thing you did today, and the *because*:" → Ledger; then the closing screen, serif: *"Court is adjourned. Anything new goes on tomorrow's docket."*).
**Streak-free by design** — reflection is the one tool with no streak at all; copy explains why: "Some tools should never become obligations. This one works whenever it's used."

## Cross-tool rules

1. Every result screen ends with exactly **one** suggested next rep — never a menu (choice overload kills follow-through at the moment of highest intent).
2. All assessment results are re-viewable, dated, and overlaid on retakes — the *delta* is always the headline, because the delta is the product.
3. Result copy never uses type-language as identity ("you ARE a Perfectionist") — always possession-language ("your dominant narrator") — the user has a pattern; the pattern doesn't have the user.
4. Every tool's scoring method is one tap away ("How this is computed") — transparency as trust.
5. Nothing shares socially by default; everything exports privately (PDF) beautifully.
