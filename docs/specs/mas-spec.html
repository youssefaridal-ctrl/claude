Product Vision

Vision: To be the most trusted, intelligent, and privacy-respecting personal finance companion for individuals who take their financial future seriously — a tool that works completely without the internet, protects every number with military-grade encryption, and explains financial behavior with the clarity of a personal advisor.

Mission Statement

Finance Bag empowers people to understand, control, and improve their financial lives through a beautifully designed, completely offline mobile application. Every peso, dollar, or dirham is tracked with precision. Every financial goal is made achievable through intelligent coaching. Every user's data remains entirely theirs — on their device, encrypted, never transmitted to a server without their explicit consent.

Ages 22–35. Earns a steady income but has never tracked spending systematically. Feels anxious about finances, has modest debt (student loans, credit cards), and no clear savings strategy. The Privacy Optimizer
Ages 30–55. High financial literacy. Deeply concerned about data privacy. Refuses cloud-first finance apps. Values offline functionality, local encryption, and complete data ownership. Primary persona for security architecture and export/backup features. Influential referral source.

Product Overview
Purpose, problem definition, business objectives, user objectives, core philosophy, and definition of success.

Problems Solved

Financial Opacity
Most people have no real-time view of their financial health. Finance Bag provides a unified dashboard, Financial Health Score, and AI explanations that make the current state of finances immediately legible — not just a transaction list.
Privacy Betrayal
Dominant finance apps monetize user data. Finance Bag eliminates this risk by design: the database lives on the device, encrypted, with no mandatory server connection. Privacy is an architectural property, not a policy promise.
Debt Paralysis
Debt is emotionally overwhelming and mathematically complex. Finance Bag models multiple payoff strategies, projects outcomes, and recommends approaches based on the user's specific debt composition — transforming paralysis into an executable plan.
Goal Abandonment
Financial goals fail without accountability systems. Finance Bag combines goal tracking, dependency modeling (cannot fund vacation before emergency fund), gamification, and AI check-ins that maintain long-term motivation through behavioral design.
Complexity Fatigue
Power-user finance apps overwhelm beginners. Finance Bag adapts its interface to user financial sophistication — simple views for beginners, full depth for advanced users — through progressive disclosure rather than feature toggling.
Localization Gaps
Finance apps are overwhelmingly English and dollar-centric. Finance Bag is designed for global users from the data model up — multi-currency at the schema level, RTL-capable from the first commit, and locale-correct formatting throughout.

Navigation Philosophy

Tab-based primary navigation with 5 tabs: Dashboard, Transactions, Budget, Goals, Settings. Secondary navigation via stack-based drill-down from any tab. Bottom sheet for quick actions (add transaction, quick budget check). No hamburger menus. Deep linking supported for notification-triggered navigation.

5-step onboarding: language select → PIN setup → profile & currency → first account → first budget template. Completable in under 3 minutes. Skip option available (returns to core setup on next prompt). Onboarding state is tracked; partial completion resumes from last step.
Add Transaction (Primary Action)
Accessible from any tab via floating action button and dashboard quick-add. Amount entry is the first field (numeric keyboard). Category, account, date are pre-filled intelligently. Notes are optional. Submission completes in 3 taps for a common expense. Total target: under 10 seconds.
Review Financial Health
Dashboard → FHS widget → detail view shows dimension breakdown. Each dimension links to the relevant feature (tap "Debt" score → debt manager). AI coach accessible from the FHS detail to explain score and suggest improvements. Journey completable in under 60 seconds.
Set Up Debt Payoff Plan

Privacy Architecture

Privacy by Design: All financial data resides exclusively on the user's device, encrypted by SQLCipher AES-256-CBC with a key derived from the user's PIN via PBKDF2 (600,000 iterations). No analytics SDK receives identifiable financial data. Crash reports are PII-scrubbed before transmission. AI interactions transmit only a structured financial snapshot (no raw transaction history). In v1.0, Finance Bag is data-silent: it neither transmits nor receives user financial data over any network.

Migration Strategy

Database migrations are numbered sequentially (migration_001, migration_002, …) and executed by Drizzle ORM on app startup. Migrations are append-only — historical migrations are never modified. Every migration has a corresponding automated test. A migration failure on app startup triggers a safe error screen with recovery options (not a crash). The migration chain is verified in CI before every release.

AI Summary
AI architecture, coaching capabilities, privacy design, provider independence, and future evolution. Full specification in SPEC-AI-001.

AI Architecture

ComponentDesign
ProviderAnthropic claude-sonnet-5. Accessed via a provider adapter interface — no direct SDK imports from domain layer. The provider can be replaced without touching coaching logic.
Financial SnapshotBefore each AI interaction, a structured snapshot builder creates a sanitized financial summary: aggregates only (no raw transaction list). Net worth, budget utilization by category, debt summary, goal progress, FHS, cashflow projection. Maximum snapshot size: 4,096 tokens.
System PromptA version-controlled, non-modifiable-at-runtime system prompt that defines the AI's role (financial coach, not advisor), disclosure requirements ("I am AI and not a financial advisor"), response format constraints, and privacy commitments.
Privacy GuaranteeIndividual transactions are never sent to the AI API. The snapshot contains aggregates and summaries only. User PII (name, account names) is anonymized before inclusion. No conversation history is sent without user-initiated context sharing.
Context Window ManagementConversation history is summarized client-side before exceeding 80% of the model's context window. Summaries are generated by the AI itself and stored locally. Full history is never discarded — it remains in local encrypted storage.

AI Coaching Capabilities

Conversational Financial Q&A
Natural language questions about the user's own finances ("Why did I overspend in June?", "Can I afford a car payment?"). Responses are contextual — grounded in the user's actual snapshot, not generic advice.
Spending Pattern Analysis
Identifies top spending categories, unusual spikes, recurring subscriptions, and year-over-year trends from transaction aggregates. Presents findings as prioritized insights rather than raw data.
Debt Strategy Guidance
Explains the mathematical difference between avalanche and snowball methods for the user's specific debt profile. Models the impact of extra payments. Frames decisions in terms of total interest saved and psychological motivation trade-offs.
Budget Calibration
Suggests budget adjustments based on 3+ months of spending history. Identifies categories where the budget is consistently under-estimated or over-estimated. Recommends rebalancing to match actual behavior while guiding toward goals.
Cash Flow Prediction
Projects income and expenses for the remainder of the current month based on recurring transactions and historical patterns. Alerts when projected month-end balance will be negative. Suggests discretionary spending reductions to prevent shortfall.
Goal Feasibility Assessment
Assesses whether a goal's target amount is achievable by its target date given current savings rate. Suggests either adjusting the timeline, increasing monthly contribution, or modifying related spending. Keeps recommendations grounded in current financial reality.

Explainability & Disclosure

Every AI response that references financial data includes a "Based on" citation noting the data points used (e.g., "Based on your last 3 months of dining expenses averaging $340/month..."). Every AI response carries a persistent disclosure label: "AI-generated. Not financial advice." The AI is instructed to recommend professional financial advisors for decisions involving taxes, investments, or legal matters.

Provider Independence

Invariant: The AI coaching system is accessed exclusively through an AIProvider interface defined in the infrastructure layer. This interface accepts a financial snapshot and returns a structured response. The domain layer has no knowledge of Anthropic, claude-sonnet-5, or any API-specific parameters. Migrating to a different provider requires implementing a new adapter only — zero changes to coaching logic, snapshot building, or UI.

SECTION 16

Strategic Recommendations

Executive-level guidance synthesized by the Principal Enterprise Solution Architect and CPO. These recommendations are forward-looking and address the highest-leverage decisions for long-term platform success.

16.1 First Release Priorities

01
Launch in One Market First
Resist the temptation to launch all 6 locales simultaneously. Soft-launch in English (US) first to validate core product-market fit before committing to localization quality at scale. Add Arabic (RTL) second as the highest-differentiation locale for the target demographic. Remaining locales in waves of two over 8 weeks post-launch.
02
Treat AI Coaching as a Differentiator, Not a Feature
The AI coaching experience is the single most powerful retention driver in the product. Invest in the quality of system prompt engineering and the coaching conversation design before launch — not as polish, but as core product work. Poor AI responses will generate negative App Store reviews that are very difficult to recover from.
03
Harden Onboarding Above All Else
The first 7-day user experience determines retention. Prioritize a delightful, frictionless onboarding flow (PIN setup → first transaction → first budget → FHS reveal) over any secondary feature. Instrument every step with analytics; optimize before the full public launch.
04
Ship Small, Ship Often
Phased rollout is your safety net. Use the 5% → 20% → 50% → 100% rollout gates aggressively. Pause at each gate and review crash-free rates and user feedback before expanding. Speed of rollout is never worth a compromised experience for your early adopters.

16.2 Post-Launch Strategic Priorities

05
Build the Longitudinal Data Moat
Your most durable competitive advantage is the depth of a user's financial history in the app. Every architectural decision that makes data migration out (and in) easier for the user paradoxically increases retention — because trust compounds over time. Prioritize CSV export quality and data portability as a trust signal, not a churn risk.
06
Gamification Must Feel Earned
The engagement layer only works if achievements feel genuinely meaningful. Audit badge unlock rates at 90 days post-launch. If ≥80% of users have a given badge within 7 days, it is too easy and loses its value. If ≤5% ever unlock it, it is too hard and invisible. Calibrate continuously.
07
Prepare the Sync Architecture Decision Early
Multi-device sync (v2.0) will require a fundamental architecture decision that touches the database schema, the encryption model, and the conflict resolution strategy. Begin the ADR process at v1.2 (6 months post-launch) so the architectural design is complete and reviewed before v2.0 engineering begins.
08
Invest in App Store ASO Continuously
App Store Optimization (ASO) — keywords, screenshot copy, localized descriptions — is a compounding investment. Allocate ½ day per sprint to ASO iteration from Day 1 of public availability. Track keyword rank weekly. This is the highest-ROI marketing channel for an indie fintech app at early scale.
