§1 Product Vision & Mission

Finance Bag is a privacy-first, offline-capable, AI-augmented personal finance application for iOS and Android. This section establishes the long-term vision that guides every delivery decision in this roadmap.

1.1 Mission Statement

Mission: Empower every person to take full control of their financial life through a beautifully designed, fully offline, encrypted personal finance tracker — where the user's data stays on their device, their privacy is absolute, and their financial clarity grows through intelligent insight and motivating design.

1.2 Target Users

The Aware Beginner

Profile: 22–32 years old. First full-time job. Knows they overspend but doesn't know where. Tried spreadsheets, abandoned them.

Primary need: Friction-free transaction logging and visual spending summaries that create self-awareness without judgment.

Success signal: Logs at least 3 transactions per week after 30 days.

The Debt Fighter

Profile: 28–42 years old. Carrying 2–5 debts (credit cards, personal loans, car). Motivated by payoff progress but overwhelmed by complexity.

Primary need: Clear debt payoff strategy visualization, progress streaks, and celebration of milestones.

Success signal: Sets up all debts and reviews payoff schedule weekly.

The Optimizer

Profile: 30–48 years old. Financially literate. Currently uses spreadsheets or a competing app. Wants deeper AI-powered insight and full data ownership.

Primary need: Comprehensive analytics, AI recommendations, export, and the guarantee that no data leaves their device.

Success signal: Reviews AI insights weekly and acts on at least one recommendation per month.

1.4 Product Principles

  1. Privacy is non-negotiable. No feature ships if it requires unencrypted transmission of financial data without explicit, granular user consent.
  2. Design for trust. Every UI decision reinforces that the user is in control: their data, their choices, their pace.
  3. Performance is a feature. A slow finance app loses users to anxiety. Sub-2.5s cold starts and 60fps scrolling are product requirements, not engineering stretch goals.
  4. Delight through simplicity. Complexity is absorbed by the architecture, never exposed to the user. One tap to log a transaction.
  5. Accessibility is required. WCAG 2.1 AA compliance is a launch criterion, not a post-launch aspiration.
  6. Reversibility over speed. Prefer the slower decision that can be undone over the fast decision that locks in a wrong direction.

§2 Development Methodology & Strategy

Finance Bag adopts an Architecture-First Agile methodology: the structural foundations are established before feature development begins, and every sprint produces a shippable internal build on that foundation.

2.1 Core Methodology Principles

Architecture-First

Phases 0–2 produce no user-facing features. They establish the technical foundation upon which all features are built. No feature work begins until the domain layer, repository interfaces, error hierarchy, CI/CD pipeline, and security layer are in place and tested.

Why: Retrofitting Clean Architecture onto a feature-laden codebase costs 3–5× more than building it right first. FinTech applications require this discipline especially for encryption and data integrity.

Risk-Driven Development

The highest-risk technical elements (encryption, SQLite performance at scale, AI API integration, biometric auth) are built and validated earliest, when schedule slack is greatest and course-correction is cheapest.

Why: Deferring risk concentrates project failure probability at the end, when recovery time is exhausted.

Incremental Delivery

Every 2-week sprint produces an installable internal build. Every phase produces a meaningful product increment that could be demonstrated to a user. No phase produces only "plumbing."

Why: Demonstrates continuous forward progress, validates assumptions with real builds, and prevents the "big reveal" failure mode.

Continuous Validation

Architecture compliance, test coverage, performance benchmarks, and accessibility audits are verified at every phase gate — not only at the end. CI pipeline enforces many of these automatically on every PR.

Why: Catches regressions while context is fresh, not months later when the source is obscure.

Controlled Scope

MVP scope is frozen at Phase 3 start. Changes to MVP scope require a formal impact assessment. A scope addition must be offset by a scope removal or a timeline extension — it cannot be absorbed by "working harder."

Why: Scope creep is the single largest cause of overruns in mobile app development. Discipline here protects launch timing.

Sustainable Pace

20% of each sprint's capacity is reserved for: technical debt reduction, refactoring, documentation, and tooling improvement. Sprints are never overloaded to "catch up." Sustainable 8-sprint velocity is worth more than heroic 2-sprint velocity followed by burnout.

Why: Technical debt accrued under deadline pressure is the primary driver of declining velocity in months 6–12.

2.3 Definition of Done (Universal)

Every user story and feature must satisfy ALL of the following before being marked complete:

  • All acceptance criteria verified and signed off
  • Unit tests written and passing; no reduction in overall coverage below floor targets
  • No new ESLint errors or TypeScript type errors introduced
  • No new dependency cycles detected by CI
  • No architecture layer violations (domain importing infrastructure, etc.)
  • Feature behind a feature flag if it is in a partial state
  • Relevant specification document updated if behaviour changed from spec
  • Accessibility: touch targets ≥ 44pt, accessibilityLabel present on interactive elements
  • Code reviewed and approved by at least one other engineer
  • Included in a deployable internal build that has been installed and smoke-tested

Exit criteria: Domain layer compiles and is fully testable in plain Node.js (no RN imports). Event bus verified with unit tests. All 8 error classes instantiated and handled in test. Migration system runs and rolls back. Logging scrubber strips test PII correctly.
2
Authentication & Security Weeks 5–6 · Sprint 3 Foundation

Delivers the security perimeter: biometric lock, key derivation, and the session lifecycle that protects all financial data.

  • Biometric authentication: Face ID (iOS), Fingerprint (Android) via expo-local-authentication
  • PIN fallback: 6-digit PIN with 5-attempt lockout and exponential backoff
  • SQLCipher key derivation tied to biometric-unlocked credential chain via Expo SecureStore
  • Session management: app-foreground re-challenge after 5 minutes backgrounded
  • Auth state machine: UNAUTHENTICATED → AUTHENTICATING → AUTHENTICATED → LOCKED
  • MOD-01 (Auth module): login, logout, biometric enrolment, PIN setup/change flows
  • Onboarding skeleton: first-run detection, initial PIN/biometric setup flow
  • 3-level error boundary setup: screen-level, root-level, global JS handler
Exit criteria: Biometric lock/unlock cycle verified on physical iOS and Android devices. SQLCipher key confirmed inaccessible after device lock. 5-attempt PIN lockout triggers correctly. Session re-challenge fires after 5-min background. Security review sign-off by tech lead.
3
Account & Transaction Engine Weeks 7–10 · Sprints 4–5 Core Feature

The primary value delivery phase. Delivers the core financial data entry experience that all other features build upon.

  • MOD-02 (Accounts): create, edit, archive account; multiple account types (checking, savings, cash, credit)
  • MOD-03 (Transactions): create, edit, delete (soft), list with keyset pagination; transfer between accounts
  • MOD-04 (Categories): default category set; custom categories; category icons; category rules
  • Money value object fully operational: integer centimes, currency-aware arithmetic, locale-aware display
  • Transaction list screen: FlashList virtual rendering, filter by account/category/date range
  • Quick-add transaction: bottom sheet with number pad, category picker, date/note fields
  • Account balance computation: real-time from transaction aggregate, not stored balance
  • Domain event bus: TransactionCreated, TransactionUpdated, TransactionDeleted events live and tested
  • SQLite schema v1: transactions, accounts, categories tables with all indexes
  • 10,000-record performance benchmark: all list queries <50ms confirmed in CI
Exit criteria: Full CRUD cycle for accounts and transactions verified. 10,000-record dataset scrolls at 60fps on P95 test device. Category assignment and filtering work. Account balance always matches transaction aggregate. Domain events fire and are received by test subscribers.
4
Budget Engine Weeks 11–12 · Sprint 6 Core Feature

Delivers the first actionable financial planning feature: budget creation, tracking, and threshold alerts.

  • MOD-05 (Budget): budget periods, per-category budget limits, rollover configuration
  • Budget vs. actual: real-time computation from transaction stream (no denormalized totals)
  • Budget progress visualization: per-category progress bars with threshold colour coding (≤50%, ≤80%, ≤95%, >100%)
  • Budget alerts: BudgetThresholdApproaching, BudgetExceeded domain events wired to notification stub

§11 Risk Management

This section documents delivery and project risks, distinct from the architectural risks in SPEC-ARC-001 §15. The risk register is reviewed monthly and updated at every phase gate.

DRISK-01 HIGH MVP Scope Creep
Description: Stakeholders or engineers add features to MVP scope during Phases 3–8, extending the timeline without equivalent scope removal.
Probability: High (common in all software projects).
Mitigation: (1) MVP scope is written, agreed, and version-controlled (this document §6). (2) Any scope addition requires a formal impact assessment (written, <1 page) that identifies what is removed or what timeline adjustment is accepted. (3) Tech Lead and Product Manager co-own scope gate — both must approve any change. (4) Scope freeze for MVP is declared at Phase 3 start; all subsequent scope discussions go to v1.1 backlog.
DRISK-02 HIGH Key Engineer Unavailability
Description: A critical engineer (Tech Lead or module owner) is unavailable for 2+ weeks during a critical-path phase, causing schedule impact.
Probability: Medium.
Mitigation: (1) Architecture documentation (SPEC-ARC-001) and ADR records ensure knowledge is not siloed. (2) Pair-programming on critical path phases (Auth, Transaction Engine) ensures two engineers know each module. (3) 3-week buffer exists at Phase 15 to absorb unplanned slippage. (4) Cross-training: each engineer reviews and contributes to at least one module outside their primary ownership per phase.
DRISK-03 HIGH Estimation Inaccuracy — Phase 3 and 10
Description: Phase 3 (Transaction Engine) and Phase 10 (AI Platform) are the two highest-complexity phases. Underestimation could push the critical path by 2–4 weeks.
Probability: Medium-High.
Mitigation: (1) Phase 3 allocated 4 weeks (2 sprints) — more than comparable scope in simpler apps, because offline-first + SQLCipher + performance benchmarks add testing complexity. (2) Phase 10 allocated 6 weeks (3 sprints) — AI integration is exploratory by nature; prompt engineering and caching require iteration. (3) Both phases have explicit performance benchmarks in their exit criteria; these are measured in Sprint 4 (not deferred to Phase 14) to surface issues early. (4) Weekly velocity check: if Sprint 4 finishes at <80% of planned scope, Phase 5–6 are re-sequenced to recover.
DRISK-04 MEDIUM Anthropic API Pricing / Availability Change
Description: Anthropic changes pricing during Phase 10 development or post-launch in a way that makes per-user AI costs unsustainable, or the API experiences extended outages.
Probability: Low-Medium.
Mitigation: (1) IAIProvider interface abstracts the provider — a pricing-driven migration to an alternative LLM (OpenAI, local model) is a configuration change, not an architectural change. (2) AI features always degrade gracefully (cached responses, static fallback content). (3) Daily rate limit (10 requests/user/day) is enforced client-side to cap per-user cost regardless of pricing. (4) Business model: AI features are in the premium tier, so per-user cost can be factored into pricing.
DRISK-05 MEDIUM App Store Rejection
Description: Apple or Google rejects the v1.0 submission, causing a delay of 1–4 weeks during Phase 15.
Probability: Low-Medium (first submissions have ~15% rejection rate).
Mitigation: (1) App Store guidelines reviewed during Phase 15 preparation (not at submission time). (2) Privacy labels and GDPR compliance implemented in Phase 13, which directly reduces privacy-related rejection risk. (3) Phase 15 timeline includes a 10-day buffer specifically for resubmission. (4) Common rejection categories (IAP compliance, privacy, content) are reviewed by tech lead before submission using the App Store Review Guidelines checklist. (5) TestFlight submission made in Week 40 (during Phase 14) to get early feedback from App Review.
DRISK-06 MEDIUM Expo SDK Upgrade Breaking Change Mid-Development
Description: A mandatory Expo SDK upgrade (security patch, breaking API change) during development introduces regressions that cost 1–2 weeks to resolve.
Probability: Medium (SDK evolves on a semi-annual cycle).
Mitigation: (1) SDK version is locked in package.json for each phase; upgrades only at phase boundaries. (2) Upgrade evaluation: changelog reviewed before each phase starts; upgrade deferred if it touches critical-path modules. (3) E2E test suite (Phases 13+) provides regression coverage to quickly identify upgrade-introduced breaks. (4) Architecture is not Expo-native-specific in domain/application layers — an SDK upgrade affects only the infrastructure and presentation layers.
DRISK-07 LOW SQLite Data Corruption (Migration Failure)
Description: A schema migration fails in production for a subset of users, leaving their database in an inconsistent state and causing data loss.
Probability: Low (with proper safeguards).
Mitigation: (1) All migrations are wrapped in a SQLite transaction — partial application is impossible. (2) Down migrations are written and CI-tested for every up migration. (3) First production deployment of any new migration reaches only 1% of users for 24 hours before wider rollout. (4) Export function available before any major schema migration is triggered — users are prompted to export first. (5) Sentry monitors for migration-related error codes with immediate alert to on-call.
DRISK-08 LOW Gamification Anti-Pattern Leakage
Description: A gamification feature ships with dark-pattern characteristics (variable-ratio schedule, FOMO mechanics, artificial urgency) that harm user wellbeing and violate SPEC-GAM-001 §8.
Probability: Low (with structured review).
Mitigation: (1) SPEC-GAM-001 §8 anti-pattern list is a mandatory Phase 12 exit criterion checklist item reviewed by both Tech Lead and Product Manager. (2) Each gamification feature requires a "behavioral design justification" note in its story description linking to a specific positive behavioral science reference from SPEC-GAM-001 §1. (3) Full opt-out is a Phase 12 launch criterion — if opt-out is incomplete, Phase 12 does not exit.

§15 Post-Launch Plan

Hotfix branch prepared. EAS Build configured for expedited release. Apple expedited review process bookmarked. Team can deploy a P0 hotfix within 4 hours of root cause identification.

  • Support inbox triage: Support requests categorised into: bug, feature request, UX confusion, documentation gap. Each category has a resolution owner.

18.2 Delivery Business Rules

BR-RDM-01
No phase may begin before the preceding phase's exit criteria have been fully verified and signed off by the Tech Lead. Phase exit sign-off is a written record (not an implicit assumption) stored in the project management tool and linked from the phase's definition in this document.
BR-RDM-02
MVP scope (§6) is frozen at Phase 3 start. Any post-freeze addition to MVP scope requires a written impact assessment (≤1 page) identifying what is removed or what timeline extension is accepted. Verbal approval is not sufficient; both the PM and Tech Lead must sign off in writing.
BR-RDM-03
Every 2-week sprint must produce a functioning, installable internal build on the final day of the sprint. A sprint that does not produce a build has failed its Definition of Done regardless of how many stories were completed. Build production is a non-negotiable sprint outcome.
BR-RDM-04
20% of each sprint's engineering capacity is reserved for technical debt reduction, refactoring, documentation, and tooling improvement. This allocation is non-negotiable and is tracked separately in the sprint board. It may not be reallocated to feature work to "catch up" on a lagging sprint.
BR-RDM-05
Architecture violations (dependency cycles, layer boundary violations, any type in non-generated code) are P0 defects that block sprint completion. They are never deferred. A PR introducing an architecture violation must not be merged; CI enforces this automatically.
BR-RDM-06
All gamification features are opt-in and subject to a mandatory anti-pattern review against SPEC-GAM-001 §8 before any gamification feature ships. If a feature cannot be implemented without anti-patterns, it is redesigned or removed — it is not shipped with a known anti-pattern and "fixed later."
BR-RDM-07
No production release may proceed without a minimum 48-hour staging soak for native builds and a 24-hour staging soak for OTA updates. The soak period is measured from successful staging deployment, not from the decision to release. Emergency hotfixes (P0) may use a 4-hour soak with explicit Tech Lead sign-off.
BR-RDM-08
The Security Hardening phase (Phase 13) is mandatory and cannot be skipped or merged with Phase 14 to save time. All High and Critical security findings from the penetration test must be resolved before the Open Beta stage gate opens. Medium findings must have a documented resolution timeline.
BR-RDM-09
Post-launch bug fixes take absolute priority over new feature development for the first 30 days after any production release. During stabilisation sprints, no new feature work is started until the P0/P1 bug count reaches zero and the P2 bug list is triaged with resolution timelines.
BR-RDM-10
Performance benchmarks must be measured on P95 representative devices (iPhone 12 / equivalent mid-range Android), not on developer machines or high-end devices. A benchmark that passes on a developer machine but fails on P95 devices is a failing benchmark. CI runs benchmarks on defined emulator configurations that approximate P95 device performance.
BR-RDM-11
The risk register (§11) is reviewed and probability/impact scores updated at every phase gate. A risk whose probability has increased since the last review triggers an immediate mitigation activation review with the full team — it is not deferred to the next monthly review.
BR-RDM-12
The critical path (§8.1) is re-evaluated at every phase gate. Any change to the critical path — a new dependency identified, a phase duration revised, a parallel opportunity discovered — triggers a delivery plan revision. The revised critical path is communicated to all stakeholders before the next sprint begins.
BR-RDM-13
Every new external dependency (library, SDK, service) added after Phase 2 requires a dependency decision note: why this dependency over alternatives, what the removal strategy is, and what the architectural boundary it operates within is. This note is part of the PR description and becomes an ADR if the dependency is a significant architectural choice.
BR-RDM-14
This document (SPEC-RDM-001) is a living document. It is formally reviewed and updated quarterly, at every phase gate, and whenever a risk materialises or a scope change is accepted. The document version is incremented on each update. An outdated delivery plan that no longer reflects reality is a project risk, not an administrative matter.
BR-RDM-15
Future version planning (§17) is a directional roadmap, not a commitment. Versions v1.1 through v6.0 are defined architecturally to confirm the current v1.0 design does not block them, but their scope, timeline, and priority are determined by actual user engagement data and market conditions after v1.0 launch — not by pre-launch assumptions made in this document.
